The 5 Most Common 1/500 Legal Bottlenecks — And How to Fix Them

Across 200+ project files assessed, LexFin Capital has identified the 5 legal bottlenecks that recur most often in 1/500 master plan projects in HCMC and surrounding areas. On average, each one delays a project 6–12 weeks when not caught early.

1. The Approved Plan Doesn't Match the Site

The 1/500 map is approved, but actual site boundaries deviate from the construction permit file — usually due to outdated surveys after local road-boundary adjustments. This error appears in 40% of the files LexFin receives.

Risk: The construction permit gets rejected and a full re-survey is required — 8–12 weeks lost.
Fix: Cross-check the updated cadastral map at the Land Registration Office before filing — a 3-day step that saves 3 months.

2. The Land Title Isn't Subdivided per the Plan

Many projects hold one master land title without plot-by-plot subdivision per the 1/500 drawings. Banks and appraisal authorities require subdivision before anything moves forward — no exceptions.

Risk: The entire loan file is frozen until subdivision completes. Current subdivision timeline in HCMC: 6–10 weeks.
Fix: Run the subdivision file in parallel from the feasibility stage — don't wait for the construction permit.

3. Land Pending Site Clearance Without an Official Decision

Some plots within the project fall under planned roads or public infrastructure with no official site-clearance decision. Developers often don't know — and still count them in total project area.

Risk: Banks exclude the clearance-affected area from collateral — actual LTV lands 15–25% below projections.
Fix: Verify site-clearance decisions at the ward-level People's Committee before valuation — a mandatory step in LexFin's assessment checklist.

4. Land-Use Purpose Not Yet Converted

CLN, ONT or BHK land not yet converted to urban residential (ODT) or commercial-service use per the 1/500 plan's designation. This is a hard precondition — banks will not disburse against unconverted land, even with an approved plan.

Risk: Developers spend on leveling and infrastructure but can't raise bank capital because the title still shows the old land use.
Fix: Complete the land-use conversion procedure in parallel with the 1/500 approval process.

5. Missing Investment Policy Approval

Under the 2020 Investment Law, projects of 20ha or more — or any commercial housing project — must obtain investment policy approval from the HCMC People's Committee before implementation.

Risk: The project can be suspended, with no legal protection for capital already invested.
Fix: Non-negotiable — this is the mandatory first step in LexFin's legal pipeline before any further advisory.

DSCR 1.2x — How Banks Actually Read This Number

DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) = Net operating income ÷ Total debt obligations for the period.

A real example: a business with VND 12bn annual revenue, VND 8bn operating costs, VND 4bn net income. A VND 20bn loan at 9%/year over 5 years → annual debt service of roughly VND 5.1bn. DSCR = 4 ÷ 5.1 = 0.78x → instant rejection.

The Real Thresholds at Vietnamese Banks

· DSCR < 1.0x: Cash flow can't cover debt → rejected, no negotiation
· DSCR 1.0x–1.2x: Grey zone — requires very strong collateral and a clean CIC history
· DSCR 1.2x–1.5x: The standard band — files are processed normally
· DSCR > 1.5x: Strong cash flow — grounds to negotiate preferential rates and higher LTV

The Most Common Mistake: Miscalculating Your Own DSCR

In practice, LexFin sees 6 out of 10 clients self-calculate DSCR at 1.3x–1.5x — only for the bank to recalculate it at 0.8x–1.0x. Why:

· Banks exclude income without valid documentation (VAT invoices, signed contracts)
· Banks add the debts you hold at other credit institutions (via CIC lookup)
· Banks apply a stress test: DSCR at interest rates 1.5–2% above current
· Some banks disregard revenue from internal or inter-company transactions

4 Steps to Optimize DSCR Before Filing

1. Standardize the last 2 years of financial statements — independently audited if possible
2. Separate project cash flow from personal and parent-company cash flow
3. Add lease agreements and long-term sales contracts as evidence of future cash flow
4. Prepare a sensitivity analysis: DSCR at +1%, +2% interest and -10%, -20% revenue scenarios

Banks don't just read today's number — they read your capacity to absorb tomorrow's risk.

Deal Structure 28/05/2026 · LFC-INS-2026-03 · 6 min read

CLN → Residential: The Real-World Land-Use Conversion Roadmap 2026

The 2024 Land Law (effective 01/08/2024) and the new land price tables have significantly changed conversion costs and procedures. Land-use fees in many HCMC areas and the outskirts are now 2–4× the old price table — the single biggest financial risk to price into your deal structure from day one.

Important: The 2025 Administrative Restructuring

Since 01/07/2025, HCMC operates a two-tier government model (city and ward/commune) — the district level no longer exists. The Department of Natural Resources and Environment has been renamed the Department of Agriculture and Environment (HCMC DAE). Verify which authority receives files for your specific location before submitting.

Step 1 — Confirm Convertibility (1–2 weeks)

Not all CLN land can be converted to residential. Mandatory checks:

· Is the plot zoned residential on the land-use planning map?
· Is it affected by planned roads, parks, or safety corridors?
· After deducting road boundaries, does the remaining area meet the minimum subdivision size?

Where to check: the ward/commune People's Committee or HCMC DAE.
Warning: Plenty of CLN plots sit squarely inside residential zoning yet still can't convert because of unpublished technical-infrastructure planning. Check in person — never rely on online maps alone.

Step 2 — File the Conversion Application (4–8 weeks)

The file includes: the conversion application, original land title, cadastral map, personal or corporate identification, and a current-status drawing.

· Households and individuals: file at the ward/commune People's Committee where the land sits
· Organizations and businesses: file at HCMC DAE

2026 note: Files submitted before 01/08/2024 but not yet approved may need supplements under the 2024 Land Law — check your file status immediately if this applies to you.

Step 3 — Settle the Financial Obligations (2–4 weeks)

Once the tax authority issues the payment notice, payment is due within the prescribed window — typically 30 days, extendable once at most.

Real numbers to budget for: In HCMC's outskirts (Binh Chanh, Hoc Mon, Cu Chi), land-use fees for a 500m² plot currently run VND 800 million – 2.5 billion depending on location. This cost is mandatory and non-negotiable — build it into the financial structure from day one.

Step 4 — Update the Land Title (2–3 weeks)

After payment, the Land Registration Office updates the land-use purpose on the title. The plot officially becomes residential land — eligible for bank mortgage and project construction.

Timeline Summary

Step 1 — Confirmation: 1–2 weeks | Survey fees VND 5–15m
Step 2 — Application: 4–8 weeks | Admin fees VND 1–3m
Step 3 — Financial obligations: 2–4 weeks | VND 800m – 2.5bn (location-dependent)
Step 4 — Title update: 2–3 weeks | Issuance fee VND 0.5–1m
Total timeline: 3–5 months

Cases that CANNOT convert: land inside irrigation-works protection corridors, protective forest land, land zoned for public works not yet cleared, and land under unresolved legal dispute.
